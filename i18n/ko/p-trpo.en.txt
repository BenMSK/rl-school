@@ 5
Maximise a surrogate objective, the old policy's advantages re-weighted by the new-to-old probability ratio, subject to the constraint that the average KL divergence between new and old policies stays below δ = 0.01, which the theory shows guarantees monotonic improvement. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05477">Schulman 2015</a> <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1502.05477">Schulman 2015</a>
@@ 6
Learning to ride a bike, you lean a little at a time. Lean too far at once and you fall, and you have to start again from where you fell, which is worse than where you began. TRPO is a rule for the computer: "each practice session, change what you would do by no more than a small, fixed amount", with a bit of maths that says why that amount is safe.
@@ 7
A policy gradient is only a local direction. Take too big a step and the policy changes, the states it visits change, and the next batch of data comes from a worse policy; the loop can collapse and not recover. The PPO paper later measured it: on seven MuJoCo tasks an unconstrained surrogate scored −0.39 on a scale where random is 0 and the best run is 1, because on HalfCheetah it ended worse than random. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347">Schulman 2017</a> Step size is also a hyperparameter you cannot set once: the same α is huge for one parameter and tiny for another. Natural gradient fixes the geometry but not the step length; in TRPO's own experiments, natural gradient without the trust region failed to produce hopping or walking. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1502.05477">Schulman 2015</a>
@@ 8
Bound how much the policy may change, measured in behaviour (KL divergence between action distributions), not in parameters. Theorem 1 gives a lower bound on the new policy's return: the surrogate improvement minus a penalty C · D<sub>KL</sub><sup>max</sup>(π, π̃) with C = 4εγ/(1−γ)<sup>2</sup>, where ε is the largest advantage magnitude. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1502.05477">Schulman 2015</a> Maximising that bound each step cannot decrease the true return. The penalty coefficient from the theory gives very small steps, so the practical algorithm swaps the penalty for a hard constraint on the <em>average</em> KL, with radius δ. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1502.05477">Schulman 2015</a>

Approximate: L ≈ g<sup>T</sup>(θ − θ<sub>k</sub>) and D̄<sub>KL</sub> ≈ ½ (θ − θ<sub>k</sub>)<sup>T</sup> H (θ − θ<sub>k</sub>), where H is the Fisher information matrix. <a class="src" href="https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/algorithms/trpo.html">Achiam 2018</a>

@@ 16
Backtracking line search: shrink the step by a factor until the measured KL is ≤ δ and the surrogate actually improved. <a class="src" href="https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/algorithms/trpo.html">Achiam 2018</a>
@@ 17
Check the arithmetic: with δ = 0.01 and x<sup>T</sup>Hx = 4 the step scale is sqrt(0.02/4) = sqrt(0.005) ≈ 0.0707 along x. If the realised KL is 0.013, backtrack to 0.8 × 0.0707, then 0.64 × 0.0707, until KL ≤ 0.01 (RL School arithmetic from the formula above).

@@ 37
TRPO turned "how big a step?" from a tuning problem into a constraint with a meaning, and it worked on neural-network policies with tens of thousands of parameters. Its direct descendant is PPO (2017), whose stated aim is "the data efficiency and reliable performance of TRPO, while using only first-order optimization". <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347">Schulman 2017</a> The idea of bounding behaviour change by KL reappears wherever policies are fine-tuned from a reference: InstructGPT's RLHF adds "a per-token KL penalty from the SFT model at each token" to keep the language model close to its starting point. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155">Ouyang 2022</a> See <a href="#p-ppo">the PPO review</a> for what replaced the constraint.
@@ 38
The theory bounds the <em>maximum</em> KL over states; the algorithm constrains the <em>average</em>, and the authors say so. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1502.05477">Schulman 2015</a> Each update needs a conjugate-gradient solve with Fisher-vector products, and the method "is not compatible with architectures that include noise (such as dropout) or parameter sharing (between the policy and value function, or with auxiliary tasks)". <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347">Schulman 2017</a> On Atari the authors "could not obtain error statistics due to time constraints". <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1502.05477">Schulman 2015</a> Reproducibility bit later: Henderson et al. split 10 identical TRPO runs on HalfCheetah into two groups of five and found them significantly different (p = 0.0016) from random seeds alone. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06560">Henderson 2018</a>
