@@ 5
Dreamer learns a compressed model of the world from replayed experience, then trains its actor and critic entirely on imagined rollouts inside that model; V3 made this work with the same hyperparameters everywhere by normalising every quantity, and Dreamer 4 scaled the model to a 2-billion-parameter transformer that learns from video.
@@ 6
Before you step onto ice you imagine slipping, and you walk differently because of it. Dreamer watches what happens when it acts, builds a small imaginary world in its head, and then practises thousands of futures there, which is much cheaper than practising for real. Dreamer 4 learned its imaginary Minecraft mostly by watching videos of other people playing, and then practised inside it until it could find diamonds, something no player manages by luck.
@@ 7
Model-free agents need huge numbers of real interactions, and every earlier model-based agent needed per-domain tuning: different reward scales, different loss weights, different horizons. DreamerV2 (2020) had reached a median of 2.15 gamer-normalised on 55 Atari games against Rainbow's 1.47, but only on Atari. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02193">Hafner 2020</a> Minecraft's diamond task, a 12-item chain with sparse rewards and episodes of up to 36,000 steps, had never been solved from scratch. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104">Hafner 2023</a>
@@ 8
Make every signal the networks see live on the same scale, so one configuration fits all domains. V3 does this with <strong>symlog</strong> transforms of inputs and targets, a critic that predicts a distribution over bins instead of a number, returns normalised by their 5th–95th percentile range, and KL losses clipped below 1 nat so they switch off once small. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104">Hafner 2023</a> Dreamer 4 keeps the imagination loop and swaps the recurrent model for a transformer trained with <strong>shortcut forcing</strong>, so it stays accurate over long rollouts and fast enough to run in real time. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24527">Hafner 2025</a>
@@ 9
<strong>World model (RSSM).</strong> An encoder maps the image x<sub>t</sub> to a discrete latent z<sub>t</sub>; a recurrent sequence model h<sub>t</sub> = f(h<sub>t−1</sub>, z<sub>t−1</sub>, a<sub>t−1</sub>) carries memory; a dynamics predictor guesses ẑ<sub>t</sub> from h<sub>t</sub> alone; heads from (h<sub>t</sub>, z<sub>t</sub>) decode the image, predict reward and predict whether the episode continues. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104">Hafner 2023</a>
@@ 10
<strong>Loss.</strong> Prediction loss (decoder, reward, continue) with weight 1; dynamics loss, pulling the prior toward the posterior, weight 1; representation loss, pulling the posterior toward the prior, weight 0.1; both KL terms floored at 1 nat ("free bits") and smoothed with 1% uniform noise. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104">Hafner 2023</a>
@@ 11
symlog(x) = sign(x) · ln(|x| + 1),  symexp = its inverse
@@ 12
<strong>Imagine.</strong> Start from replayed latent states and roll the dynamics predictor forward T = 16 steps, choosing actions with the actor. No pixels are produced. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104">Hafner 2023</a>
@@ 13
<strong>Actor-critic.</strong> The critic predicts a categorical distribution over exponentially spaced bins with two-hot regression; returns are divided by max(1, S) where S is the 5th–95th percentile range; the actor uses the REINFORCE estimator for both discrete and continuous actions. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104">Hafner 2023</a>
@@ 14
<strong>Dreamer 4.</strong> A ~400M-parameter tokenizer compresses frames; a ~1.6B block-causal transformer (spatial attention within a frame, temporal attention once every 4 layers) predicts the next tokens given interleaved actions. Shortcut forcing trains it on noised representations conditioned on noise level and step size and predicts the clean representation, so inference needs only K = 4 forward passes per frame. Three phases: pretrain on video (and actions when available), fine-tune policy and reward heads by behaviour cloning, then imagination training with the transformer frozen and a sign-of-advantage objective (PMPO). <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24527">Hafner 2025</a>

@@ 31
With one set of hyperparameters, V3 covered 8 domains: Atari (57 games), ProcGen (16), DMLab (30), Atari100k (26), Proprio Control (18), Visual Control (20), BSuite (23 environments, 468 configurations) and Minecraft, "over 150 diverse tasks", outperforming the tuned specialist on each benchmark summary. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104">Hafner 2023</a> Across six model sizes from 12M to 400M parameters, "increasing the model size directly translates to both higher task performance and a lower data requirement". <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104">Hafner 2023</a> Every Dreamer run discovered diamonds in Minecraft within 100M environment steps, with no human data or curriculum, on one A100 GPU for 9 days. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104">Hafner 2023</a> The ablations rank the KL objective as the most important robustness technique, then return normalisation and the two-hot critic. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104">Hafner 2023</a>

@@ 34
Dreamer 4 trained on the 2,541-hour VPT contractor dataset (video plus actions) and never touched the game during training; it reaches diamonds in 0.7% of 60-minute episodes and the iron pickaxe in 29%, a task that takes humans about 20,000 to 24,000 mouse and keyboard actions. A fine-tuned VPT gets as far as sticks 53% of the time and a Gemma-3 vision-language-action baseline reaches the iron pickaxe in 11%. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24527">Hafner 2025</a> The authors call this "100x less data" than VPT's 270K hours of YouTube video; training ran on 256 to 1024 TPU-v5p chips, and the finished world model runs interactively in real time on a single GPU. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24527">Hafner 2025</a>
@@ 35
DreamerV3 was the first general RL algorithm whose selling point was "no tuning", and its 2025 Nature publication made world models respectable as a route to generality rather than a curiosity. <a class="src" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08744-2">Hafner 2025a</a> DayDreamer (2022) had already put the loop on hardware, with a quadruped walking in about 1 hour without a simulator. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14176">Wu 2022</a> Dreamer 4 ties the line to video-trained generative models: if the model can be learned from recorded play, the agent can be trained before it ever acts, which is the setting robots live in. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24527">Hafner 2025</a>
@@ 36
Imagination is capped at 16 steps because model errors compound, so the agent cannot plan long chains explicitly; it relies on the critic to summarise them. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104">Hafner 2023</a> V3 spends capacity reconstructing pixels, which MuZero and TD-MPC2 avoid (<a href="#p-muzero">the MuZero review</a>). Dreamer 4's diamond rate is 0.7%, a proof of existence rather than a reliable skill, and its "100×" counts only labelled hours. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24527">Hafner 2025</a> As of October 2026 there is no independent replication of the offline diamond result.

Q3. Why does Dreamer stop imagined rollouts at 16 steps?
@@ 47
