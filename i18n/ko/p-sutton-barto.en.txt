Sutton and Barto define reinforcement learning as "learning what to do—how to map situations to actions—so as to maximize a numerical reward signal", then show how to do it exactly with tables (Part I), approximately with function approximation (Part II), and what it has to do with brains, psychology and real systems (Part III). <a class="src" href="http://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf">Sutton &amp; Barto 2018</a>
@@ 6
Imagine a thick guide for teaching a puppy tricks when nobody tells the puppy the rules, only when it gets a treat. The first part is for small games where you can keep score on one sheet of paper. The second part is for games with so many situations that you need a clever guesser instead of a sheet. The third part asks how real animals, and real brains, do the same thing. The two authors put the whole guide online for free.
@@ 7
Before this book, the ideas of RL lived in three separate literatures. Psychology had Thorndike's Law of Effect (1911), the "essence of trial-and-error learning". Control theory had Bellman's dynamic programming and Markov decision processes (1957). A third thread, <strong>temporal-difference learning</strong>, ran from Samuel's checkers player (1959) to Sutton's 1988 paper. The three were "fully brought together" only in Watkins's 1989 thesis on Q-learning. <a class="src" href="http://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf">Sutton &amp; Barto 2018</a> The book's job is to teach the unified subject with one notation: return G<sub>t</sub>, discount γ, values v<sub>π</sub>(s) and q<sub>π</sub>(s,a), dynamics p(s′,r|s,a). The second edition is the one cited here; the authors' page lists the full PDF, errata, code and slides. <a class="src" href="http://incompleteideas.net/book/the-book-2nd.html">Sutton &amp; Barto 2018</a>
@@ 8
Two sentences carry the book. First, every estimate is updated the same way: <strong>NewEstimate ← OldEstimate + StepSize [Target − OldEstimate]</strong> (eq. 2.4); bandits, TD(0), Sarsa, Q-learning and semi-gradient methods only differ in what the target is. Second, <strong>generalised policy iteration</strong>: let policy evaluation and policy improvement interact at any granularity, because "almost all reinforcement learning methods are well described as GPI". <a class="src" href="http://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf">Sutton &amp; Barto 2018</a>

The lineage of this Paper Bible runs through the book. Watkins's Q-learning (§6.5, 1989) is the update inside DQN (Nature, February 2015), which the book covers as a case study in §16.5. <a class="src" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14236">Mnih 2015</a> REINFORCE (§13.3, Williams 1992) is the ancestor of A3C, TRPO and PPO. <a class="src" href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00992696">Williams 1992</a> The authors are still setting the agenda: Sutton co-wrote the Alberta Plan (2022) and, with Silver, "Welcome to the Era of Experience" (2025), which argues agents "will inhabit streams of experience, rather than short snippets of interaction". <a class="src" href="https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Era-of-Experience%20/The%20Era%20of%20Experience%20Paper.pdf">Silver &amp; Sutton 2025</a>
@@ 66
The book stops in 2018. The phrases "trust region" and "proximal policy" do not occur in it, so the whole of <a href="#d8">Day 8</a> (TRPO, PPO, SAC) is taught from the papers. <a class="src" href="http://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf">Sutton &amp; Barto 2018</a> Deep networks appear mainly in the Chapter 16 case studies; Part II is built around linear features and tile coding. One slip to know: §16.5 says DQN reached human level on "29 of the 46 games", where the Nature paper reports 49 games. <a class="src" href="http://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf">Sutton &amp; Barto 2018</a> <a class="src" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14236">Mnih 2015</a> For the post-2018 arc (DQN → PPO/SAC → MuZero → offline RL → RLHF and reasoning models) a 2026 book-length treatment exists. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00133">Bista 2026</a>
