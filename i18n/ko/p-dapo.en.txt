@@ 5
GRPO as written collapses its own exploration, wastes compute on questions where every sample agrees, under-weights long answers and punishes truncation randomly; DAPO fixes each with one change and shows the ladder of gains. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476">Yu 2025</a>
@@ 6
Same classroom as <a href="#p-deepseek-r1">DeepSeek-R1</a>: 16 students answer a question and get compared with the class average. DAPO adds four house rules. If everyone gets a question right or everyone gets it wrong, swap it out, it teaches nothing today. Let rare good ideas grow a bit faster than usual. Grade every sentence equally, not every student equally, so long careful answers count. And warn a student who is running out of paper before cutting them off.
@@ 7
When R1 appeared, the recipe was described but the training details were not, and the authors of DAPO report that naive GRPO on Qwen2.5-32B reached only 30 on AIME 2024 and suffered <strong>entropy collapse</strong>, reward noise and training instability. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476">Yu 2025</a> Entropy collapse means the policy's distribution sharpens early; the 16 samples become near-copies, the group has no variance, and the advantages go to zero (see the worked example in <a href="#d12">Day 12</a>).

@@ 11
<strong>Clip-Higher.</strong> PPO's symmetric clip ε = 0.2 caps how fast a rare token can grow: a token at probability 0.01 can reach at most 0.012 in one update, while a token at 0.9 can move to 1.0. DAPO decouples the bounds, ε<sub>low</sub> = 0.2 and ε<sub>high</sub> = 0.28, so low-probability tokens get room and entropy stays up. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476">Yu 2025</a>
@@ 12
<strong>Dynamic sampling.</strong> Drop prompts whose G = 16 samples are all right or all wrong (zero advantage, zero gradient) and keep sampling until the batch is full of informative prompts. That is the constraint under the expectation. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476">Yu 2025</a>
@@ 13
<strong>Token-level loss.</strong> GRPO averages per response then across responses, so a 10,000-token answer and a 100-token answer weigh the same and each token in the long one barely counts. DAPO divides by the total token count Σ|o<sub>i</sub>| instead, so long reasoning is weighted in proportion and long rambling gets penalised in proportion. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476">Yu 2025</a>
@@ 14
<strong>Overlong reward shaping.</strong> Truncated answers used to get the same −1 as wrong ones, which is noise. Soft punishment: a 4,096-token buffer before the 16,384-token expected maximum (20,480 total) in which the penalty grows with length; first they simply masked truncated samples out of the loss. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476">Yu 2025</a>
@@ 15
<strong>Setup.</strong> No KL term, because a long-CoT policy legitimately drifts far from its initialisation; rule-based reward on the DAPO-Math-17K dataset, whose answers were rewritten as integers so a string match is the verifier; prompt batch 512, learning rate 1×10<sup>−6</sup>; built on verl. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476">Yu 2025</a>

@@ 31
DAPO was the first complete, reproducible RLVR system at the 32B scale: algorithm, verl training code and the DAPO-Math-17K dataset, released together in March 2025. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14476">Yu 2025</a> Its four fixes spread quickly. Hugging Face TRL made the token-level loss its default <code>loss_type</code> and recommends ε<sub>high</sub> = 0.28; <a class="src" href="https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/grpo_trainer">Hugging Face 2026</a> ProRL kept dynamic sampling and Clip-Higher (with ε<sub>high</sub> = 0.4) for 2,000+ steps; <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24864">Liu 2025</a> and the ScaleRL study (<a href="#p-scalerl">review</a>) used DAPO as a named baseline recipe and kept zero-variance filtering, its cousin of dynamic sampling. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13786">Khatri 2025</a>
@@ 32
There is no limitations section. The evaluation is maths only, on one base model, with one metric; the authors say the method "can be readily transferred to other tasks" but do not test it, and they leave the study of emerging reflective behaviour to future work. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476">Yu 2025</a> Removing the KL term is asserted, not ablated. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476">Yu 2025</a> A month later a value-based counterpoint from the same lab, VAPO, reached 60.4 on the same setup in 5,000 steps with a critic and no crashes, so the critic is expensive rather than useless. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05118">Yue 2025</a> And ScaleRL found that DAPO's loss reached a lower asymptote than CISPO or GSPO when compute was scaled. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13786">Khatri 2025</a>
