A3C: Asynchronous advantage actor–critic

Sixteen CPU threads, no GPU and no replay memory: an actor–critic that beat the Atari state of the art in half the training time of the GPU-trained DQN family.

@@ 5
Run many copies of an actor–critic agent in parallel threads, each in its own environment, and let them push gradients into one shared network without waiting for each other; the diversity across threads replaces DQN's replay memory, and the result "succeeds on a wide variety of continuous motor control problems as well as on a new task of navigating random 3D mazes". <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.01783">Mnih 2016</a>
@@ 6
Sixteen kids play the same video game on sixteen screens. Each one has a copy of a shared notebook of tips. Every few moves a kid writes what they learned into the one shared notebook, then copies the newest notebook back. Nobody waits for anybody. Because the kids are in different parts of the game at any moment, the notebook never gets stuck on one situation.
@@ 7
DQN stabilised deep Q-learning with a replay memory and a target network, but at a cost: a million stored frames, an off-policy algorithm (replay cannot be used with on-policy actor–critics), and days of GPU time: 8 days on a GPU for DQN, 4 days on 100 machines for the distributed Gorila. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1602.01783">Mnih 2016</a> Policy-gradient methods (<a href="#p-reinforce">the REINFORCE review</a>) could handle continuous actions but, trained online from one agent, saw the same correlated, non-stationary data that broke naive deep Q-learning.
@@ 8
Decorrelate the data in <em>space</em> instead of in <em>time</em>. Instead of one agent remembering old transitions, run 16 actor–learners at once; at any instant they are in different states of different episodes, so the updates arriving at the shared parameters are already a mixed sample. The paper builds four asynchronous algorithms this way (one-step Q, one-step Sarsa, n-step Q, and advantage actor–critic); the actor–critic one, <strong>A3C</strong>, performs best. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.01783">Mnih 2016</a>
@@ 9
A global network holds policy parameters θ and value parameters θ<sub>v</sub>; the two heads share every non-output layer: conv 16 filters 8×8 stride 4, conv 32 filters 4×4 stride 2, 256 fully connected units, then a softmax over actions and a scalar V. The LSTM variant adds 256 LSTM cells. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1602.01783">Mnih 2016</a>
@@ 10
Each of 16 threads copies the global parameters, then acts in its own environment for up to t<sub>max</sub> = 5 steps or until the episode ends. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1602.01783">Mnih 2016</a>

The thread applies its gradients to the global parameters asynchronously, lock-free in the Hogwild! style, through a shared RMSProp (decay 0.99), then re-syncs and continues. γ = 0.99. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1602.01783">Mnih 2016</a>

After one CPU-day A3C's mean (344.1%) matched Dueling Double DQN's eight GPU-days (343.8%). Table 2 gives the speed-up from 1 to 16 threads averaged over seven games: 24.1× for one-step Q, 12.5× for A3C, so the asynchronous updates were not just parallel but, for the Q-methods, super-linear. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1602.01783">Mnih 2016</a>

@@ 43
A3C made deep RL affordable: a multi-core desktop instead of a GPU cluster. Its k-step advantage estimator is the λ = 1 end of the GAE family, and the PPO paper notes its own advantage estimator reduces to A3C's when λ = 1. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347">Schulman 2017</a> The synchronous, batched twin, <strong>A2C</strong>, became the standard policy-gradient baseline; PPO's Atari comparison is against A2C and ACER, and the entropy weight 0.01 carried straight over. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347">Schulman 2017</a> The paper also showed the same code learning TORCS driving, MuJoCo continuous control and a 3D maze with an LSTM policy, the first broad demonstration that one on-policy deep method spans discrete and continuous control. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1602.01783">Mnih 2016</a>
@@ 44
Means flatter medians: after one day A3C's median was 68.2%, below Double DQN's 110.9%, because a few games with huge normalised scores dominate the mean. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1602.01783">Mnih 2016</a> The comparison is in wall-clock time, not frames: on-policy data is used once and thrown away, so A3C is sample-hungry. The 2017 PPO paper found that A2C "won" only 1 of 49 Atari games against PPO's 30 on average reward over training. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347">Schulman 2017</a> And asynchrony itself makes runs hard to reproduce; Henderson et al. (2018) showed deep policy-gradient results shifting significantly from random seeds alone. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06560">Henderson 2018</a>
