@@ 3
A Q-learning regulariser that pushes Q down on the actions the policy likes and up on the actions in the dataset, so the learned value lower-bounds the truth: 2–5× the return of prior offline methods on mixed data, and 61.2 on antmaze-medium-play where every baseline scored 0.0.

@@ 5
CQL adds one term to the ordinary Bellman loss, α times (soft-maximum of Q over all actions minus average Q over dataset actions), which makes the Q-function pessimistic exactly where the data is silent, and this is enough to turn SAC into a strong offline learner with "only 20 lines of code".
@@ 6
You learn to cook only from a recipe book and cannot taste anything until the exam. A careless cook thinks "chocolate in the soup could be amazing!" just because the book never says it is bad. CQL's rule: if the book never tried it, assume it is worse than it looks, and trust the recipes the book did use a bit more. You end up combining the best bits of many so-so recipes instead of inventing disasters.
@@ 7
<strong>Offline RL</strong> learns from a fixed dataset collected by some behaviour policy, with no further interaction. Standard off-policy methods fail here because the Bellman target max<sub>a'</sub> Q(s', a') queries actions the data never covered; their values are guesses, the max picks the most over-estimated guess, and the error propagates. Fujimoto et al. named this <em>extrapolation error</em> in 2018. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02900">Fujimoto 2018</a> In CQL's own tables, SAC trained offline scores 3.5 on hopper-mixed and 1.9 on walker2d-mixed, below plain behaviour cloning (11.8 and 11.3). <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.04779">Kumar 2020</a>
@@ 8
Do not constrain the policy; make the Q-function pessimistic. If Q's expected value under the learned policy is a <em>lower bound</em> on the true value, a policy that maximises it cannot be fooled by phantom values on unseen actions. The regulariser is a soft-max over all actions minus the mean over dataset actions: non-negative, and small only when unseen actions are not valued above seen ones.
@@ 9
<strong>Penalise the policy's actions.</strong> Equation 1 adds α·E<sub>s~D, a~μ</sub>[Q(s,a)] to the Bellman error, with μ the distribution of actions being evaluated. Theorem 3.1: the result lower-bounds the true Q pointwise, for large enough α. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.04779">Kumar 2020</a>
@@ 10
<strong>Give back on dataset actions.</strong> Equation 2 subtracts α·E<sub>a~π̂<sub>β</sub></sub>[Q(s,a)]. The bound is now on the policy's expected value, not pointwise, and is tighter (Theorem 3.2). <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.04779">Kumar 2020</a>
@@ 11
<strong>Choose μ by a regulariser.</strong> CQL(R) maximises over μ with a regulariser R(μ); using entropy gives CQL(H), the version used in the experiments. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.04779">Kumar 2020</a>

@@ 13
Check it by hand with three actions and Q = [1, 2, 5], where only action 2 appears in the data: log(e<sup>1</sup> + e<sup>2</sup> + e<sup>5</sup>) ≈ 5.07, minus 2 gives a penalty of 3.07, almost all of it from the unseen action with Q = 5. If that action's value is pulled down to 1.5, the penalty falls to log(e + e<sup>2</sup> + e<sup>1.5</sup>) − 2 ≈ 0.68.

@@ 24
On the D4RL gym tasks (normalised so 0 is random and 100 is expert), CQL(H) scored 48.6 on hopper-mixed against 25.3 for the best prior method (BEAR), 26.7 on walker2d-mixed against 11.3 (BC), and 98.7 on walker2d-medium-expert against 11.3; its Table 1 caption says it "greatly outperforms prior methods with complex distributions". <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.04779">Kumar 2020</a> It did not win everywhere: on hopper-medium-expert, BC scores 111.9 to CQL's 111.0. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.04779">Kumar 2020</a> On antmaze-medium-play, where the agent must stitch together segments of undirected trajectories, CQL(H) reached 61.2 while BC, SAC, BEAR, BRAC-p and BRAC-v all scored 0.0. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.04779">Kumar 2020</a> On Adroit, CQL variants "are the only methods that improve over BC". <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.04779">Kumar 2020</a> On Atari with 1% of the DQN replay data, Q*bert went from 383.6 (QR-DQN) to 14,012.0. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.04779">Kumar 2020</a>
@@ 25
CQL made offline RL a one-line change to an existing algorithm, and it became the baseline every later method is measured against: IQL (October 2021) beat it on AntMaze, 378.0 total to 303.6, by never querying unseen actions at all, while tying on locomotion (692.4 to 698.5). <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06169">Kostrikov 2021</a> Decision Transformer (June 2021) reported its numbers against CQL (<a href="#p-dt">the DT review</a>). <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.01345">Chen 2021</a> In <a href="#d10">Day 10</a> it is the canonical answer to "why not just run DQN on the dataset".
@@ 26
Pessimism costs performance on clean expert data (hopper-medium-expert), and α must be set or dual-tuned. The authors leave "a rigorous theoretical analysis of CQL with deep neural nets" to future work and flag that offline methods can overfit, so finding "simple and effective early stopping methods" is open. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.04779">Kumar 2020</a> CQL still evaluates Q on unseen actions in order to penalise them; IQL's expectile trick avoids that query entirely, and the D4RL-gym tasks it was tuned on later stopped separating methods, which motivated OGBench in 2024. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20092">Park 2024</a>
