Because the best KL-regularised policy for any reward can be written as the reference policy times exp(reward/β), you can run the equation backwards, read the reward off the policy, and train the policy directly to rank the human-preferred answer above the rejected one. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290">Rafailov 2023</a>
@@ 6
In <a href="#p-instructgpt">InstructGPT</a> you first build a robot judge that copies the real judge, then practise against the robot. DPO skips the robot. It reads the judge's old scorecards ("this answer beat that one") and nudges you straight toward the winners and away from the losers, a little harder on the pairs you currently have backwards.
@@ 7
RLHF with PPO is expensive and fiddly. Four copies of a large model sit in memory (policy, value model, reference, reward model), the loop needs fresh samples from the policy at every step, and PPO is sensitive to hyperparameters. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290">Rafailov 2023</a> The paper's abstract calls the standard pipeline "complex and often unstable". <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290">Rafailov 2023</a> Most of that machinery exists only to turn a dataset of pairwise preferences into a policy.
@@ 8
Start from the RLHF objective (Eq. 3): maximise expected reward minus β times the KL divergence from the reference π<sub>ref</sub>. Its optimum is known in closed form (Eq. 4):
@@ 9
π<sub>r</sub>(y|x) = (1/Z(x)) · π<sub>ref</sub>(y|x) · exp( r(x,y) / β )
@@ 10
Z(x) sums over every possible response, so it cannot be computed. Invert it (Eq. 5):
@@ 11
r(x,y) = β·log( π<sub>r</sub>(y|x) / π<sub>ref</sub>(y|x) ) + β·log Z(x)
@@ 12
Plug this into the Bradley–Terry preference model. Both answers in a pair share the prompt x, so they share Z(x), and it <strong>cancels</strong> in the difference r(x,y<sub>w</sub>) − r(x,y<sub>l</sub>). The reward model has vanished; what is left is a loss on the policy. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290">Rafailov 2023</a>
@@ 13
<strong>Data.</strong> A fixed dataset of (prompt x, preferred y<sub>w</sub>, rejected y<sub>l</sub>) triples, usually produced by sampling from an SFT model and asking humans to choose. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290">Rafailov 2023</a>
@@ 14
<strong>Two models.</strong> The trainable policy π<sub>θ</sub>, initialised from the SFT model, and a frozen copy π<sub>ref</sub>.
@@ 15
<strong>Loss</strong> (Eq. 7), minimised by ordinary gradient descent:
@@ 16
L<sub>DPO</sub>(π<sub>θ</sub>; π<sub>ref</sub>) = −E<sub>(x,y<sub>w</sub>,y<sub>l</sub>)~D</sub>[ log σ( β·log π<sub>θ</sub>(y<sub>w</sub>|x)/π<sub>ref</sub>(y<sub>w</sub>|x) − β·log π<sub>θ</sub>(y<sub>l</sub>|x)/π<sub>ref</sub>(y<sub>l</sub>|x) ) ]
@@ 17
<strong>Read the gradient.</strong> Each β·log(π<sub>θ</sub>/π<sub>ref</sub>) is an <strong>implicit reward</strong> r̂<sub>θ</sub>. The gradient raises log π<sub>θ</sub>(y<sub>w</sub>|x), lowers log π<sub>θ</sub>(y<sub>l</sub>|x), and weights the pair by σ(r̂<sub>θ</sub>(x,y<sub>l</sub>) − r̂<sub>θ</sub>(x,y<sub>w</sub>)): how wrongly the implicit reward currently ranks it. The paper notes an unweighted version degenerates. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18290v3">Rafailov 2023</a>
@@ 18
<strong>Check by hand.</strong> β = 0.1, winner log-ratio +0.5, loser log-ratio −0.3: argument 0.1·0.8 = 0.08, σ(0.08) = 0.520, loss 0.654. Far from zero, so DPO keeps pushing until the margin is large. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18290v3">Rafailov 2023</a>

Three tasks. Controlled sentiment on IMDb with GPT-2-large: DPO reaches a strictly better reward-vs-KL frontier than PPO, even PPO given the ground-truth reward. Summarisation on Reddit TL;DR: about 61% win rate against human reference summaries at temperature 0, versus about 57% for PPO at its best temperature, judged by GPT-4. Single-turn dialogue on Anthropic HH with Pythia-2.8B: the only method to beat the preferred completions in the dataset. The paper did not meaningfully tune β for DPO and evaluates models "up to 6B parameters". <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18290v3">Rafailov 2023</a>

@@ 24
DPO made preference tuning cheap enough for anyone with two copies of a model and a preference file, and became the default alignment step in open-weight releases through 2023–24. It also sharpened the vocabulary: "RLHF" is a KL-regularised objective, and DPO showed that solving it does not require RL. The counter-move came within a year: on-policy methods that do sample, such as RLOO (February 2024), beat DPO on TL;DR 77.9 vs 66.6 win rate with k = 4 samples. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14740">Ahmadian 2024</a> And the 2024–25 reasoning wave needed a verifier's 0/1 on fresh samples, which DPO cannot use (<a href="#p-deepseek-r1">the DeepSeek-R1 review</a>).
