How to use this course
@@ 23
<strong>One day per sitting.</strong> Read the lesson top to bottom. Each one is self-contained but builds on the day before. Week 1 builds the classical machine; Week 2 goes from deep RL to the research frontier.
@@ 24
<strong>Play with every widget.</strong> The widgets are small, real computations running in your browser: a bandit that actually learns, a grid that actually converges, a PPO objective you can bend. Drag the sliders until the idea clicks.
@@ 25
<strong>Look at the architecture.</strong> Every method gets a diagram of its parts and what flows between them (networks, buffers, environments, workers). If you can redraw the diagram from memory, you understand the method.
@@ 26
<strong>Take the quiz, then mark the day done.</strong> Progress is saved on this device only. Nothing is sent anywhere.
@@ 27
<strong>Open the sources.</strong> The small chips after sentences (like this one <a class="src" href="http://incompleteideas.net/book/the-book-2nd.html">Sutton &amp; Barto 2018</a>) link to the original book chapter, paper, blog, or documentation. When you finish the course, read the originals in the <a href="#papers">Paper Bible</a>.
@@ 28
If you have done <a href="https://benmsk.github.io/llm-school/">LLM School</a>, you already know what a neural network, gradient descent, and a Transformer are; this course assumes that and nothing more. Equations are shown and explained, never proved. Code snippets are ten lines or fewer.
@@ 29
The map
@@ 30
Week 1 builds the machine: rewards, bandits, Markov decision processes, Bellman equations, dynamic programming, learning from experience, Q-learning and DQN, policy gradients. Week 2 is the modern toolkit and where it is used: PPO and SAC, world models, offline RL, exploration and self-play, RL for language models and agents, RL in robots and industry, and the open problems.

About the sources
@@ 41
Every lesson was researched against primary sources (the Sutton &amp; Barto textbook, the original papers, official lab blogs and documentation, and university courses) and checked on 2026-10-25. Fast-moving facts (which model is best, benchmark scores, what frontier labs are doing) are marked "as of October 2026" and will age. If you spot an error, <a href="https://github.com/BenMSK/rl-school/issues">open an issue</a>.
