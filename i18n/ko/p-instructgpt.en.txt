InstructGPT: training language models to follow instructions with human feedback
@@ 3
Three steps (supervised fine-tuning, a reward model trained on human rankings, PPO against that reward model) turned GPT-3 into a model whose 1.3B-parameter version humans preferred over the original 175B GPT-3, "despite having 100x fewer parameters". <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155">Ouyang 2022</a>

Taught in <a href="#d12">Day 12</a> · Read time of the original: ~50 min (the main text; the appendices double it)
@@ 5
Humans cannot write a reward function for "helpful, honest and harmless", but they can say which of two answers is better; InstructGPT trains a network to imitate those judgements and then uses PPO, with a KL leash to the starting model, to make the language model score higher on it. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155">Ouyang 2022</a>
@@ 6
Picture a spelling-bee judge who cannot tell you the right word but can always say which of two attempts sounded better. First you build a robot that copies the judge's taste. Then you practise against the robot thousands of times, doing more of what it likes. One rule: you may improve, but you may not turn into a completely different kid overnight. That rule is the KL leash.
@@ 7
GPT-3 predicted the next token of internet text. Asked "Explain the moon landing to a 6-year-old", it was as likely to produce more questions as an explanation. The paper's framing is that the language-modelling objective is <strong>misaligned</strong> with what users want: bigger models did not become more helpful, and they still produced untruthful, toxic or unhelpful output. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155">Ouyang 2022</a> Learning a reward from pairwise human comparisons had already worked for Atari and simulated locomotion in 2017, using feedback on less than 1% of the agent's interactions. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03741">Christiano 2017</a> InstructGPT applied it to a 175B-parameter language model.
@@ 8
Treat the language model as a policy (<a href="#d12">Day 12</a>): state = prompt plus tokens so far, action = next token, one reward at the end of the response. Replace the missing reward function with a <strong>reward model</strong> learned from human rankings, and optimise it with PPO (<a href="#p-ppo">the PPO review</a>) while penalising distance from the supervised starting point so the policy cannot wander into regions where the reward model is wrong. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155">Ouyang 2022</a>
@@ 9
<strong>SFT.</strong> About 40 contractors wrote demonstrations for roughly 13k prompts; GPT-3 is fine-tuned on them with ordinary supervised learning. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.02155">Ouyang 2022</a>
@@ 10
<strong>Reward model.</strong> For about 33k prompts, labelers rank K = 4 to 9 sampled outputs. A 6B model (the paper only ever uses 6B reward models; the 175B one trained unstably) learns a scalar r<sub>θ</sub>(x, y) with the Bradley–Terry loss (Eq. 1): <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.02155">Ouyang 2022</a>
@@ 11
loss(θ) = −1/C(K,2) · E<sub>(x,y<sub>w</sub>,y<sub>l</sub>)</sub>[ log σ( r<sub>θ</sub>(x, y<sub>w</sub>) − r<sub>θ</sub>(x, y<sub>l</sub>) ) ]
@@ 12
Each ranking yields C(K,2) pairs; dividing by that count gives every prompt equal weight. Check it by hand: winner scored 2.0, loser 0.5 gives σ(1.5) = 0.818 and loss 0.201; backwards, σ(−1.5) = 0.182 and loss 1.70. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.02155">Ouyang 2022</a>
@@ 13
<strong>PPO.</strong> On about 31k prompts, sample a response, score it with r<sub>θ</sub>, and update with PPO. The objective (Eq. 2, "PPO-ptx"): <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.02155">Ouyang 2022</a>
@@ 14
objective(φ) = E<sub>(x,y)~π<sub>φ</sub></sub>[ r<sub>θ</sub>(x,y) − β·log( π<sub>φ</sub>(y|x) / π<sup>SFT</sup>(y|x) ) ] + γ·E<sub>x~pretrain</sub>[ log π<sub>φ</sub>(x) ]
@@ 15
The β term is a per-token <strong>KL penalty</strong> toward the frozen SFT model. The γ term mixes in pretraining loss so the model keeps its general skills; plain "PPO" has γ = 0. Four networks live in memory: the policy and PPO's value model (trained), the reference and the reward model (frozen). <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.02155">Ouyang 2022</a>

<a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155">Figure 2, Ouyang 2022</a> The canonical RLHF pipeline. Step 1 is supervised learning on human-written answers; step 2 trains a model to reproduce human rankings; step 3 is RL against that model. Only step 3 is RL.
@@ 18
Labelers preferred the 175B InstructGPT over 175B GPT-3 about 85% of the time (±3%), and preferred even the 1.3B PPO-ptx model over the 175B GPT-3. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.02155">Ouyang 2022</a> On closed-domain tasks InstructGPT hallucinated about half as often (21% vs 41%), and produced about 25% fewer toxic outputs when told to be respectful; the toxicity gain vanished without that instruction and bias benchmarks did not improve. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.02155">Ouyang 2022</a> Plain PPO regressed on some public NLP benchmarks (the "alignment tax"); PPO-ptx reduced the regression but still lagged GPT-3 on DROP, SQuADv2 and translation. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.02155">Ouyang 2022</a>

@@ 21
InstructGPT is the recipe behind ChatGPT (November 2022) and, with small changes, behind most assistant models since. It also set the cost structure everyone then tried to escape: four large models in memory and a slow sampling loop. DPO (May 2023) removed the reward model and the sampling (<a href="#p-dpo">the DPO review</a>). <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290">Rafailov 2023</a> GRPO (2024–25) removed the value model and, for maths and code, replaced the learned reward with a verifier (<a href="#p-deepseek-r1">the DeepSeek-R1 review</a>). <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948">DeepSeek-AI 2025</a>
@@ 22
The authors say the models "are neither fully aligned nor fully safe": they still fabricate facts and follow harmful instructions. The preferences come from about 40 mostly English-speaking contractors, most comparisons were labelled by a single person, and the data is almost all English, so "aligned" means aligned to that group. <a class="src" href="https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.02155">Ouyang 2022</a> Later work found two systematic reward-model failures: preference data favours answers that agree with the user (sycophancy), and reward gains in RLHF mostly track response length. <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548">Sharma 2023</a> <a class="src" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03716">Singhal 2023</a>
